# The Art of War — strategic analysis

Use the book as a **diagnostic tool**, not as decorative quotation. Every principle used must turn into a concrete recommendation for the user's case.

Full reference of the 13 chapters: [references/mind-map.md](references/mind-map.md) — read it before analyzing.

## Modes

| Request | What to deliver |
|---|---|
| "mind map", "book summary" | The Mermaid map from `references/mind-map.md` + offer to generate PNG/PDF/HTML |
| Concrete situation where the user is a party (negotiation, competitor, dispute) | Analysis in the 5 steps below |
| Third-party dispute (election, competitors against each other, someone else's conflict) | **Observer mode**, below |
| "Explain chapter X" | Core ideas of the chapter + 2 business applications |

## Situation analysis — 5 steps

1. **Assessment (ch. 1) — the 5 factors**, in a table, us × the other side:
   - **The Way**: alignment/morale of the team and allies
   - **Heaven**: timing, deadlines, conditions (interest rates, seasonality, elections, new law)
   - **Earth**: terrain — market, contract, jurisdiction, bargaining position
   - **The Commander**: who decides, competence, blind spots
   - **Method**: structure, cash, processes, documentation
   Conclude: who has the advantage and where.

2. **Know the enemy and yourself (ch. 3 and 13)** — list what is **known** and what is **still missing** about the other side. State how to obtain the missing information (legitimately: public records, history, questions during the negotiation). Do not invent case data; if it is missing, say so.

3. **Win without fighting (ch. 3)** — before any confrontation, evaluate in order:
   thwart the other side's strategy → split alliances → direct contest → litigation/price war (last resort, ch. 2: a long conflict drains cash).

4. **Position and maneuver (ch. 4–8)**
   - What makes us **invulnerable** first (protections, guarantees, BATNA)
   - Where the other side is **weak/unguarded** (ch. 6)
   - **Direct and indirect** options (ch. 5)
   - Risks in our own commander: the 5 faults (recklessness, cowardice, hot temper, pride, excessive attachment — ch. 8)

5. **Plan** — 3 to 5 prioritized actions, each with: source principle (chapter), concrete action, deadline, risk. Close with the **warning sign** that would change the plan.

## Observer mode — third-party disputes

Use when the user **is not a party** to the dispute, or when the topic is political/electoral. Same 5 steps, with these changes:

1. **Mandatory symmetry** — every table has one column per side (A × B, or more sides), with the same level of detail and the same rigor for each. No "us × enemy".
2. **No cheering** — do not recommend which side should win or take sides. Politics and elections: describe what each side *needs* to do to win, never what the user should support or vote for.
3. **Data first** — start from verifiable numbers (official results, financial statements, market share). If the user has the data on their machine, use it and cite the source. Subjective classifications (e.g., "the right-wing camp") are marked as such.
4. **Explicit gaps** — list what is missing (polls, endorsements, abstention, unpublished financials) and do not fill it with assumptions.
5. **Closing** — instead of the "plan", deliver the **3 decisive points** (what will decide the dispute, with source chapter) and the **warning sign** that would change the reading.
6. **Offense** — negative campaigning, disinformation or personal attacks never appear as a recommendation; ch. 12 ("do not act out of anger") applies to both sides.

Conclude with "advantage to A/B in X, because Y" — an assessment, not a preference.

## Rules

- Answer in English, directly, with tables.
- Do not romanticize conflict: if the best outcome is an agreement, say so.
- Deception (ch. 1) in business = **not revealing your position**, never lying, defrauding or acting illegally.
- When there is a contract, read the document and anchor the analysis in the actual clauses (deadlines, penalties, guarantees, termination).
- Short, paraphrased quotations; the focus is on application.
